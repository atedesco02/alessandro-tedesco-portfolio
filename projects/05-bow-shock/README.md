# Earth's bow shock

**MSc course project — Politecnico di Milano — 2025/2026**
Advisor: Prof. G. Gori. Individual project. 

## Summary

A theoretical, literature-based study of the Earth's bow shock as a **collisionless
MHD shock** — the standing shock that forms where the hypersonic solar wind
decelerates against Earth's magnetosphere, treated as a blunt obstacle. The report
analyses the fluid-dynamic properties of the shock and, in particular, the
dissipation mechanisms that operate in a plasma where ordinary viscosity is absent.

## Scope & approach

- Extends classical compressible-flow shock theory (Rankine–Hugoniot relations,
  Mach number, oblique-shock geometry) into the magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) and
  collisionless-plasma regime.
- Synthesis of the space-plasma-physics literature, anchored by characteristic
  solar-wind data at 1 AU.

## What it covers

- Solar wind properties and the frozen-in interplanetary magnetic field (Parker
  spiral, ≈ 45° at 1 AU).
- Plasma waves — Alfvén, magnetosonic and ion-acoustic modes — and the associated
  Mach numbers.
- MHD Rankine–Hugoniot jump conditions and shock reference frames (normal-incidence
  and de Hoffmann–Teller).
- Shock geometry (angle θ_Bn: parallel / perpendicular / oblique) and fast vs slow
  shocks.
- The bow shock as a fast-magnetosonic shock: free-stream Mach ≈ 8, magnetosonic
  Mach ≈ 5–6, density and field compression ~×4 (strong-shock limit), Knudsen
  number ~10⁶ confirming the collisionless regime.
- Collisionless dissipation: the foot–ramp–overshoot current structure,
  reflected-ion and electron-drift currents, and wave–particle heating
  (Landau damping).

## What it demonstrates

Command of compressible-flow and shock theory — Rankine–Hugoniot relations,
Mach-number analysis, oblique-shock geometry — carried into an advanced MHD/plasma
context, together with the ability to distil a substantial technical literature
into a coherent analysis. Complements the applied CFD and experimental work with
theoretical breadth in high-speed / compressible flow.

## Topics & methods

`compressible-flow & shock theory` · `Rankine–Hugoniot (MHD)` · `magnetohydrodynamics`
· `plasma physics` · `analytical modelling` · `literature synthesis`
